I'm Emily Pillmore. I'm an engineer and a mathematician, in that order on most days and the other way round on the good ones. I write about type theory and functional programming, learning machines and optimisation, mathematics, and the strange future the three of them are converging on.

Most of what I've built in public is Haskell. I maintain around forty packages on Hackage under the name [`topos`](https://hackage.haskell.org/user/topos): `base64`, `base16`, `base32`, `nonempty-vector`, `smash`, `group-theory`, and a long tail of smaller things that exist because I needed them once and it seemed rude not to publish. `vitrea`, which derives optics from the profunctor representation theorem rather than postulating them, has been a work in progress for longer than I care to say. I run the `cohomolo-gy` research org, which started as somewhere to put the reading I kept sending people — the foundational Haskell papers, the optics literature, the collected Lawvere — and has stayed that.

I was the first CTO of the Haskell Foundation. Before that I was at Kadena, where I designed the Pact smart contract language and led the core team building it. I've served on the program committee for the ICFP Haskell Implementors' Workshop, in 2021 and again in 2025. My degree is a BS in Mathematics from the University of Utah, where I spent most of my time in topology and geometry, which is where this site gets its name.

What holds my attention now is learning machines: optimisation, the mathematics sitting under reinforcement learning, and the question of what these systems are actually doing in the cases where they work. I came up in a field where you prove the thing before you ship it, and I am finding the encounter with one where you mostly do not both uncomfortable and extremely interesting. There is real mathematics down there and a great deal of folklore on top of it, and sorting one from the other is most of what I do for fun.

Mathematics is the part that does not feel like a career. It is a way of seeing, and it is the reason any of the rest of this is legible to me.
