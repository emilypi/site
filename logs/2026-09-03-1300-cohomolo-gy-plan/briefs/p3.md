### Post B — `src/content/posts/bellman-fixed-points.md` (draft, ~1600–2200 words)

Working title: *Bellman's operator is a contraction, and that is the whole game.* An essay for a technical reader that connects reinforcement learning to the Banach fixed-point theorem and to how a functional programmer thinks about recursion. Content that must be mathematically correct (check every statement):

- MDP `(S, A, P, R, \gamma)` with `0 \le \gamma < 1`; the Bellman optimality operator `(T v)(s) = \max_a \big[ R(s,a) + \gamma \sum_{s'} P(s'|s,a)\, v(s') \big]`.
- `T` is a `\gamma`-contraction in the sup norm: `\|Tv - Tw\|_\infty \le \gamma \|v - w\|_\infty` (give the short proof using `|\max_a f(a) - \max_a g(a)| \le \max_a |f(a) - g(a)|`).
- Banach: a contraction on a complete metric space has a unique fixed point and iteration converges geometrically; `\|v_k - v^*\|_\infty \le \gamma^k \|v_0 - v^*\|_\infty`.
- Value iteration as `iterate bellman v0`, with a Haskell sketch (a small, total, compiling-in-spirit example: `Map` from states to `Double`, `bellman :: MDP -> (S -> Double) -> (S -> Double)`, `converge eps = ... until (\(a,b) -> dist a b < eps) ...`). Keep it honest: mention that it is a toy with finite `S`.
- The FP angle: fixed points are how we already think (`fix`, least fixed points of functors, `Mu`), and the contraction gives you what the lattice order does not — a rate. One paragraph on how the same theorem sits under policy evaluation and under Q-learning's expected update, and one honest paragraph on where it stops helping (function approximation breaks the sup-norm contraction; cite the deadly triad by name, no citations needed beyond naming Sutton & Barto).
- Close with something felt, not a summary.
- `math: true`, `toc: true`, `lede:` a two-sentence hook. Tags: `reinforcement-learning`, `mathematics`, `haskell`. `draft: true`.
